Here are a few outside-the-frame angles that might loosen this up:

**1. Air traffic control and Bloomberg Terminal — density as an earned tool, not a flaw to fix**
ATC screens and Bloomberg terminals face the identical shape of problem: expert users, life-or-money-critical scanning speed, small displays, months-long ramp-up to fluency. Neither tries to make the live instrument beginner-friendly. ATC trainees learn on separate simulators before touching live traffic; Bloomberg treats its notorious learning curve as a retention moat, not a UX bug, and puts its onboarding investment into curriculum, not interface simplification. You might try: stop treating "3 months to fluency" as a design failure to solve on the same screen, and instead build a dedicated training/ramp surface (progressive column reveal, guided drills) that's explicitly *not* the live dispatch view veterans use. That could relieve pressure to compromise the real tool.

**2. Chess-expertise research — the bottleneck might be pattern recognition, not column count**
De Groot's classic chess studies found masters don't read the board square-by-square, they perceive chunked patterns instantly; novices decode piece-by-piece. Your veterans probably aren't reading 18 columns of text either, they're pattern-matching row *shapes* for exceptions. If that's right, the lever isn't hide-vs-show columns (which breaks their scan and explains the revolt) — it's making exception-patterns more pre-attentive: color bands, tiny glyphs, sparklines, or shape cues that let a trained eye catch anomalies without parsing digits. That could cut new-dispatcher ramp time by teaching pattern recognition faster, without removing a single column veterans rely on.

**3. Game HUD design — density as a per-player saved setting, not one fixed layout**
Competitive games solve near-identical tension (dense at-a-glance info, small screens, split-second reads) by making HUD complexity a personal, saved profile rather than one global design decision — new players get a scaffolded default that reveals more over time; veterans dial theirs to max density. Worth trying: instead of one layout that has to satisfy both camps, make column set/type size a saved per-dispatcher preset with a few built-in tiers (new-hire, standard, veteran-dense). That reframes "roomier layout vs revolt" from a binary redesign fight into a settings problem.

**4. Salesforce-style role-based page layouts / theatrical staging — decouple the demo from the tool**
Enterprise software often shows entirely different page layouts of the *same underlying data* depending on role (admin dense view vs. rep simplified view) without touching the data model. Car dealerships do something similar — the showroom floor model isn't the stripped trim customers actually drive off with. Sales could demo a curated "starter" preset built specifically to not terrify small fleets, distinct from what your two enterprise accounts actually run day to day — solving the lost-deals problem without requiring any change to the veteran-approved dense screen at all.
