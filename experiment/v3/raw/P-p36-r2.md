A few angles from outside logistics/routing that might be worth poking at:

**1. Restaurant no-show playbook, for the "not home" failures**
What I noticed: your "customer not home" problem is structurally identical to restaurant no-shows, which used to run 10-20% before OpenTable-era fixes. What worked there wasn't better prediction, it was changing the customer's commitment. Restaurants added: a card-on-file hold, a same-day reconfirmation text with a one-tap "still good?" reply, and a standby list that backfills a cancelled slot within hours instead of days. Worth trying: a reconfirmation text 2 hours before the stop (not just a morning reminder), and a short waitlist of nearby customers your crews can pivot to when a slot falls through, so a no-show becomes a swap instead of dead mileage.

**2. Pre-op / pre-flight fit-check, for stairwell and elevator failures**
What I noticed: "will the sofa fit" and "did we reserve the elevator" are both irreversible-commitment problems that surgery and aviation solved the same way, by moving the verification earlier and making it someone's explicit job rather than an assumption. Surgeons confirm implant sizing before the patient is under anesthesia; pilots run a checklist before pushback, not during. Right now your fit/elevator check seems to happen implicitly (or not at all) until the crew is standing in the doorway. Worth trying: a mandatory 60-second video call or a stairwell-width photo from the customer at booking time, plus a hard system block on scheduling any building with a freight elevator until the reservation slot is confirmed, not just requested.

**3. Rideshare live-narrowing windows, for the afternoon slippage and complaint spike**
What I noticed: your 4-hour windows behave like the old "your table will be ready between 7 and 9" model, which creates a cliff, everything's fine until suddenly it's not, and by 2pm the whole day's drift has compounded. Delivery apps replaced fixed windows with a number that tightens as the day goes ("14 stops away" → "3 stops away") so the customer's expectation updates continuously instead of breaking at a deadline. Worth trying: even a low-tech version, an automated "you're now 3rd in line, ETA updated to 3:40" text triggered off crew GPS, might convert the 2pm complaint spike into a non-event, since most complaints are about surprise, not lateness itself.

**4. Rental-car/Airbnb photo check-in, for the unprovable damage claims**
What I noticed: "we can't prove it either way" is the exact problem rental car counters and Airbnb solved with mandatory timestamped photos at both ends of the handoff, not for prevention, but so liability has evidence instead of he-said-she-said. Right now your crews probably only document damage after a claim is already filed, which is too late. Worth trying: make a stairwell/doorway photo (before and after) a required step in the crew's app before they can close out a delivery, the same way a rental return can't finalize without the photo. That alone might not cut damage, but it should cut disputed claims fast.

One more, lower-confidence, since you mentioned it in passing: the peak-weekend crew shortage is the same problem Instawork/TaskRabbit exist to solve, a pool of pre-vetted, on-call gig labor you tap only when volume spikes, rather than trying to build permanent bench strength for a few weekends a quarter. Might be worth a look if the weekend crunch is a recurring cost.
