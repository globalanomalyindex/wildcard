A guide-dog puppy socializer works through a genuinely short, high-stakes window: pups get raised and exposed to the world during roughly weeks 8-16, and a specialist doing scent-discrimination groundwork spends that window teaching a pup to hold a target scent through a noisy environment. The obsession that defines the work: catch what's going wrong *while the window is still open*, because behavior installed sloppily in that period doesn't retrain cleanly once it closes.

That headspace turned up three things in your setup.

**1. The wall switch is context-locked, not just fragile.**
A puppy trained a response only in the exact room and cues it learned in will lose that response the instant the context shifts — the fix isn't "train harder," it's making sure the core, safety-relevant behavior doesn't route through a single fragile pathway in the first place. Your bedroom switch has the same shape: it only "knows" how to control the lights by going through the Pi and Node-RED. When that context drops, the behavior doesn't degrade, it vanishes.

Concrete move: check whether your Zigbee coordinator (zigbee2mqtt or deCONZ/Phoscon) supports direct device-to-device binding for that specific switch-and-bulb pair. Most Zigbee wall switches and relays can be bound at the network layer so the switch drives the bulb directly, with no hub or automation runtime in the loop at all — in zigbee2mqtt this is one bind action in the frontend UI (or the `zigbee2mqtt/bridge/request/device/bind` API). Set that single binding up first. It turns "lights stop responding when the Pi dies" into a solved problem permanently, and it's a config change, not a rebuild.

**2. Every week you don't write it down is a week closer to losing it for good.**
Good groundwork that isn't captured the day it happens is the single most common way it gets lost once the window closes — no amount of later effort reliably reinstalls it. You're already down to "half the flows," and you've had two corruptions eat understanding you couldn't regenerate.

Concrete move: first hour of your next session, do exactly one thing — Node-RED's Menu -> Export -> all flows to a file, `git init` a small repo for it, commit, and write one plain-English sentence per flow describing what it's for and why. That commit log is worth more than your year-old disk image, because it's diffable and readable, not just restorable — the next corruption becomes a `git checkout`, not a memory test.

**3. Four hours is a short-rep budget, not a marathon-session budget.**
Nobody runs a long unstructured exposure session with a young pup — the discipline is short, single-target reps, logged, then stop, because trying to cover everything in one sitting is how a session burns out without landing anything durable.

Concrete move: split your four hours into two bounded reps instead of one big push. Rep one (this weekend): the Zigbee bind above, plus the flows.json export and commit — nothing else. Rep two (next weekend): pick exactly one flow you don't understand, disable every other flow so it fires alone, trigger it once, and read only its own debug output. That isolation is the same discipline as testing a target scent against clutter — you can't tell what one flow does while ten others are also firing on the same MQTT topics. Write a one-line comment on what you learn, then stop for the week.
