I sharpen saw chains for a living — filing the cutters, but more to the point, filing the depth gauges (rakers) that sit just in front of each cutter and set how deep it's allowed to bite. My eye is on energy-and-flow: how the saw's power gets metered, tooth by tooth, into the wood around one continuous loop.

A few things from my bench that genuinely rhyme with your nav problem:

**1. Sharpness-per-tooth isn't the same gauge as bite-of-the-whole-chain.**
In my shop, if you file a cutter back without lowering its depth gauge to match, that tooth is technically "sharp" but the chain cuts *less* over time — because the cutter's edge now sits higher relative to the raker in front of it, and the raker blocks the bite. A tooth-by-tooth sharpness check will never catch this; it's a whole-loop property, invisible from any single cutter.

Your usage log is exactly a per-tooth sharpness check — "this item gets used daily" — and it's structurally incapable of detecting bloat, because bloat isn't a per-item defect. It's a second, separate quantity: total friction the whole loop imposes on someone traversing it once. Concrete move: track that second number directly — clicks or decision-points a new self-serve signup takes to reach first meaningful action — set an explicit quarterly budget for it, and let *that* number, not per-item DAU, be the actual criterion for what gets collapsed or removed. An item can pass its own usage check and still be the thing blowing the budget.

**2. There's no universal raker height — it's set to the wood, not the chain.**
A depth gauge tuned right for ripping hardwood is dangerously over-aggressive for bucking softwood. I never set one gap and expect it to serve both cuts; I condition the gauge to the material in front of me.

You have two different cuts running through one shared bar: dense enterprise workflows and a first-pass self-serve signup. Concrete move: give them different defaults on the same nav instead of forcing one setting to serve both — a curated 5-6 item default for accounts under some seat threshold or under 90 days old, with the rest one level down, auto-expanding to the full 13 the moment an account's usage crosses into an enterprise profile (seat count, or first touch of an acquisition-specific module). Nothing gets removed for anyone who's using it; the depth gauge just gets set per account instead of globally.

**3. Touch one tooth's raker in isolation and the whole loop chatters.**
If I lower one cutter's depth gauge without touching its neighbors, that tooth bites harder than the rest, the chain pulls sideways in the cut, and you get vibration and lead — exactly the kind of localized damage a spot-fix produces. The fix is never a single tooth; it's a full pass across a batch, done together, in one sitting.

Your last nav change was a single-item spot-move, and it produced textbook chatter: three enterprise escalations in a week. That's not proof nav changes are unsafe — it's what a lone touched tooth does to a loop that was never recalibrated as a set. Concrete move: the next change shouldn't be another single item — it should be one bundled, pre-announced pass: e.g., collapse the 9 settings tabs into 4 in a single release, or fold 2-3 acquisition-derived top-nav items into one submenu at once, shipped together, with advance notice to the specific large accounts flagged as heavy users of the touched items and an explicit rollback path.

That's what genuinely held up on my bench — happy to pull any one of these further, or step back out and leave you to it.
