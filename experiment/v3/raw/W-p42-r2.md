I'm a conservation geneticist. My days are spent matching seized elephant tusks back to the population they were poached from, using a DNA reference map built across confirmed African herds — and reuniting split pairs: the two tusks from one animal that traffickers deliberately route through separate ports and separate paperwork, because a shared multilocus genotype can't be laundered the way a shipping manifest can. The thing I fear most in casework isn't a missing sample. It's trusting a channel that was built to be gamed — a manifest, a GPS log — when there's a channel sitting right there that can't lie.

Three things from that world that rhyme with your closet Pi.

**1. Don't let the fragile layer carry the load-bearing signal**
In my casework, origin never rides on the crate's paperwork — that trail is designed to be falsified. It rides on the genotype, because that's the one layer that can't be re-routed or relabeled by whoever's moving the goods. Your wall switch has the same problem in reverse: right now, flipping it has to survive a round trip through the SD card and Node-RED before a bulb responds, so when that one fragile layer corrupts, the "independent" manual control goes down with it — it was never actually independent. Many Zigbee wall switches support direct binding to a bulb or bulb group at the radio level, skipping the coordinator entirely. Bind the bedroom switch straight to the bulbs, then test it for real: pull power on the Pi and confirm the switch still turns the lights on and off. That's the one fix your partner will actually feel.

**2. Stop taking snapshots, start keeping a running record**
With a backlog of hundreds of tusks and a handful of technicians, we never rely on periodic re-checks — degradation doesn't wait for your schedule, so evidence gets captured continuously, not resurveyed once a year. Your one-year-old disk image is a periodic snapshot of a system that changes constantly. Two small, mostly-automated moves close that gap without eating your four hours: turn on Node-RED's built-in Projects feature so every flow save is committed to a local git repo (five minutes, then it's passive), and add one cron job that images the SD card to a USB drive or NAS weekly. Neither requires you to understand the flows first — they just mean the next corruption costs you days, not a year.

**3. When you don't know what a sample is, don't guess — instrument it**
An unlabeled tusk doesn't get a guessed origin; it gets tested against the reference map, or it waits. When a flow feels like a stranger's work, resist the urge to reconstruct its intent from memory. Node-RED's debug node can be dropped onto just the input and output of one suspect flow at a time and left running for a day of normal household use — you'll see the actual trigger events and actual outputs it's producing, ground truth instead of a guess. Build a running one-page table as you go: flow name, trigger, action, confidence (known / confirmed-by-debug / still-unknown). Four hours a weekend gets you maybe two or three flows deep — that's fine, the table is the point, not finishing it in one sitting.
