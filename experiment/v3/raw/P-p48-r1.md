Here are a few angles from outside the moving-logistics frame that might be useful:

**1. Theory of constraints (manufacturing/ops) — find the one true bottleneck, not the longest list**
Forty accounts, a mover date, an ISP backlog, and a passport in transit all *feel* like one big pile, but they don't all have the same shape. The passport renewal is the one item that's genuinely time-locked and irreversible (you can't call ahead and change when the mail arrives). Everything else — most of the 40 accounts, voter record, non-urgent subscriptions — can be updated any day in the next few months with zero penalty. Try sorting the whole list into "has a hard external clock" (passport, ISP install, lease overlap) versus "just needs to happen eventually" (most accounts). Then plan your five weeks almost entirely around the first group and let the second group wait until after the dust settles.

**2. Software indirection — don't migrate every reference, redirect the alias**
In codebases, when a value is hardcoded in forty places, you don't hunt down all forty call sites — you introduce one layer of indirection that all of them point to. USPS mail forwarding is exactly that layer for your address: file it early and it becomes a 12-month buffer that catches anything you haven't gotten to yet, including the passport. That reframes "update 40 accounts before the move" into "update them whenever, because the forwarding order is quietly catching the gap."

**3. Blue-green deployment — treat the 6-day overlap as a designed cutover window, not a scheduling accident**
When engineers switch traffic from an old system to a new one, they deliberately run both in parallel for a window, routing things over gradually and keeping a rollback path open until the new one's proven. Your 6-day lease overlap is that window, and it's also your only mover slot — which is actually convenient, not just tight. Instead of experiencing it as pressure, you could use it on purpose: move the big stuff early in the window, keep the old place minimally live as fallback, and don't force electric/internet to flip on day one — dual-service (old apartment's utilities plus a mobile hotspot) through the ISP's three-week backlog is a normal "keep the old system running until the new one's confirmed" move, not a failure.

**4. One-way doors vs. two-way doors (Bezos framing) — for the synchronous stuff, front-load the irreversible calls**
Most of the account updates are two-way doors — easy to redo if you get them slightly wrong or late. The ISP install slot and anything needing a live 9-6 phone call are closer to one-way doors, since backlogs and appointment slots don't forgive lateness. Worth identifying up front which handful of calls truly require a live human during business hours (probably just the ISP and maybe the DMV) versus which can go through an async channel — online forms, email, chat, or even a written/notarized authorization letting someone else act on your behalf — so your limited lunch-break minutes go only to the irreducibly synchronous ones.
