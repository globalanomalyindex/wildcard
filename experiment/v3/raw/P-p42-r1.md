Here are a few angles that sit outside the usual "back up the SD card better" frame.

**1. Borrow from aviation's fail-operational design.**
Fly-by-wire aircraft don't just have redundant computers, they have a hard rule: when the smart system dies, the pilot still has *some* physical path to control the plane. Your actual problem isn't backup cadence so much as coupling: the wall switch's most basic function has been routed entirely through a general-purpose computer with a habit of corrupting its own storage. You might get more relationship peace from decoupling the critical path than from perfecting the backup. Even a cheap trick, like wiring the switch so it retains direct/local control (many Zigbee wall switches can pair directly to bulbs or a hub without needing Node-RED for the basic on/off case) buys you graceful degradation: the Pi can die and the room still works, only the clever automations go away.

**2. Borrow from chaos engineering (Netflix's old "break it on purpose" philosophy).**
Right now the SD card corrupting is an ambush each time, a surprise event that eats a weekend. Chaos engineering treats expected failure as a scheduled, boring event you rehearse rather than survive. One weekend, deliberately kill the Pi (pull power mid-write, whatever tends to trigger the corruption) with a fresh backup strategy in place, and time your own recovery. You'll learn exactly where your process breaks *on your terms*, in a two-hour block instead of during an argument with your partner.

**3. Treat the two-year-old flows like inherited legacy code, not your own memory.**
You're not just "forgetting" your flows, you're in the position of a developer who inherited someone else's undocumented codebase, except that someone is past-you. The usual move there isn't to re-derive understanding from scratch under time pressure; it's to annotate opportunistically. Each weekend, before touching anything, spend ten minutes adding comment nodes to whatever flow you're looking at, purely descriptive, no fixing. In a few sessions the system becomes legible again without ever costing you a dedicated "understand everything" weekend.

**4. Borrow "cattle, not pets" from infrastructure engineering.**
The DevOps shift that ended a lot of this exact pain was deciding a server shouldn't be a fragile, nursed-back-to-health individual (a pet) but a disposable instance you can regenerate from a definition (cattle). Right now your Pi is very much a pet: the real "it" lives in your memory and a stale disk image, not in something you could hand to a stranger and say "reproduce this in twenty minutes." The reframe: instead of imaging the *whole SD card* periodically, put the parts that matter (Node-RED flow JSON, zigbee2mqtt config) under version control in a small git repo, synced automatically. A corrupted card then becomes a fresh SD card flash plus a git pull, a chore, not a reconstruction project.
