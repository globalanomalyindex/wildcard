Looking at this from outside logistics-optimization framing:

**1. It's an information problem disguised as a routing problem.** The failures you list (not home, won't fit stairwell, elevator not reserved) are all things that were *knowable before the truck left the warehouse* — nobody just checked. That reframes the fix: instead of routing software, a mandatory pre-departure "clearance" step (confirm window by text, confirm dimensions against a stairwell photo/measurement on file, confirm elevator reservation) could kill most of the 10% failure rate for the cost of a checklist, not a system. Worth asking: which of your three failure modes is actually a *verification-timing* problem rather than a *scheduling* problem?

**2. Borrow the "day-of surgery" trick from healthcare/aviation: a same-day reconfirmation call, not a booking-time confirmation.** Surgeries and flights don't just confirm at scheduling — they reconfirm hours before, when circumstances (traffic, no-shows, weather) are actually knowable. Airlines overbook precisely because they've modeled which slots will no-show; you could do the reverse — a 7am robocall/text "your sofa arrives 12-4, reply Y" — which directly targets the "not home" third of your failures and is cheap to pilot on ten stops before rolling out.

**3. Damage claims as a photography problem, not a training problem.** Insurance adjusters and rental-car companies solved "he-said-she-said" not with better care but with mandatory photo documentation at handoff (30-second walk-around before/after). If your crews photograph the stairwell and sofa condition at pickup and drop-off — even just phone photos — the 3% claims number likely doesn't drop, but your liability exposure and dispute time might collapse, which may be the actual cost you're trying to solve for. Worth checking whether "3% damage claims" is really "3% damage" or "3% we can't disprove."

**4. Gig-economy crew-sourcing (Uber/Instacart model) for the vetted-crew shortage.** Rather than treating peak-weekend crews as a hiring problem (background check → onboard → deploy), some furniture/moving companies now maintain a pre-vetted pool that's activated on-demand rather than employed — the vetting happens once, well before peak, decoupled from the scheduling crunch. If the bottleneck is "we can't vet fast enough when we need them," the fix might be moving vetting off the peak-weekend timeline entirely rather than doing more of it faster.
