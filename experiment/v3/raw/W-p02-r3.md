Thinking about your cluster upgrade as a physicist who works on entanglement, specifically its constraints and failure modes, here's where that lens actually caught something.

**1. The webhook outage is a decoherence problem, not a workload problem**

In entangled systems, decoherence isn't caused by every stray interaction with the environment. It's caused specifically by coupling to something that touches the *whole* system at once. One such coupling can collapse the entire state in a single event, while a thousand purely local disturbances would leave most of it intact.

Your admission webhook is exactly that kind of universal coupling: the one piece of the pipeline that intercepts every pod's scheduling decision cluster-wide. A misconfiguration there isn't "one more thing that can break," it's the one thing whose failure mode is instant and total, which is exactly what happened last time.

Concrete move: on the next attempt, deploy any change to that webhook (or any new one the version bump requires) with `failurePolicy: Ignore` and a short timeout, on a single canary node pool first. Require a fixed clean observation window (48 hours of real traffic, say) before flipping it to `Fail` cluster-wide. Hold that one component's promotion bar categorically stricter than any per-workload rollout, since it's the only piece with fleet-wide blast radius.

**2. Your local-PV services need teleportation, not relocation**

When a quantum state can't be copied and can't be physically carried elsewhere (no-cloning forbids both), the actual technique for moving it is teleportation: pre-share a resource between source and destination *before* anything risky happens, destroy the original in a measurement, then send a small correction so the destination reconstructs it exactly. The state is never "in transit," it's rebuilt from a pre-established copy plus a verified correction step.

Your stateful services pinned to local PVs have the same shape: you can't just detach a disk and carry it to a new node safely.

Concrete move: for each stateful, node-pinned service, before touching the node at all, stand up a replicated destination volume on the target node using whatever replication the app supports (app-level sync, litestream, a scheduled snapshot). Let it fully catch up, then cut over via a scripted, verified failover (stop writes, confirm checksums match, flip the pointer), rather than relying on Kubernetes' drain/reschedule to carry the local PV along. Pilot this on one stateful service as a template before generalizing to the rest.

**3. Your eight teams are being asked to violate monogamy**

Entanglement is monogamous: a system maximally correlated with one partner cannot be simultaneously correlated with a third. Split it across many and every relationship degrades to weak, classical correlation, not a fair fraction of the strong kind.

Eight teams with little spare capacity, split across three clusters at once, are structurally in that weak regime. Someone half-helping three migrations at once doesn't yield a third of the value on each, it yields close to none on any of them.

Concrete move: for the EOL cluster's 10-week window, name exactly one point of contact per owning team, committed to that migration alone, and explicitly freeze that person's involvement in the other two clusters' upgrade work until this one ships. Treat "I'll help across clusters as time allows" as a known anti-pattern to reject, not a reasonable way to stretch scarce time.
