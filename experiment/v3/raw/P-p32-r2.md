A few angles from outside the "fix the schedule" frame, worth poking at:

**1. The CSA box / farm-share model (perishable-goods economics)**
Farm shares and fish CSAs solve almost your exact problem: fixed, low-variance product, high cost of guessing wrong, customers who want to change their minds at the last minute. Their answer isn't better guessing, it's a *default standing order* that ships unless the customer opts out by a real deadline (usually 48-72 hours out, not same-day). Worth trying: give each cafe a default weekly order based on their trailing average, auto-confirmed unless they change it by, say, 6pm two days prior. Late-night texts become exceptions to a baseline instead of the entire order arriving in a scramble. This alone might explain a chunk of your 8% waste, since right now every single loaf is a same-day guess.

**2. Theory of Constraints (manufacturing) — the two ovens are your "drum"**
In Goldratt's framework, when a system has one true bottleneck (your ovens), everything else — mixing, proofing, cold-room timing — should be scheduled as a "buffer" protecting the bottleneck's throughput, not the other way around. Right now it sounds like the cold room and proofing space are competing for the same window because the plan flows forward from "when orders come in" rather than backward from "when the ovens need to be full." Worth trying: map backward from oven capacity and lock the laminated-dough/proofing conflict into two non-overlapping blocks by design, rather than letting it be resolved ad hoc each night.

**3. Ship's night-order book / pilot's flight strips (externalizing tacit expertise)**
Captains who won't be on the bridge overnight, and controllers handing off a shift, don't rely on the next person reconstructing their mental model — they write a compact, standardized artifact (night orders, flight strips) that encodes just the decisions that matter for the next few hours. Your head baker holding the whole schedule in his head is the single point of failure blocking him from cutting back nights. Worth trying: before he drops a night, have him externalize the *decision rules* he's applying (not just the schedule) — e.g., "if cafe X hasn't confirmed by 9pm, default to last week's count minus 10%." That's a transferable artifact, not just a spreadsheet.

**4. Job scheduling on shared compute (contention windows)**
Two ovens and one cold room being fought over by bread and laminated dough is structurally the same problem as two processes contending for one CPU/GPU in computing. The standard fix isn't a smarter scheduler reacting in real time — it's reserving fixed blocks (a "commit" time after which the resource allocation is locked) so processes stop thrashing each other. Your 10pm order cutoff into an 11pm mix start is already close to this, but the cold-room/proofing overlap suggests the *reservation*, not just the order deadline, needs to be locked earlier and treated as non-negotiable capacity, the same way a scheduler won't let a low-priority job pre-empt one that's already running.
