Drawing on charcoal, and specifically its material properties, three things surfaced that seem worth handing you.

**1. Pyrolysis is staged, not one-step.**

Good charcoal comes from wood that's first cured of its moisture and volatile gas under restricted oxygen, and only once that's driven off is it allowed to char. Combine those two stages, burn a green log straight through, and you get a fire that either quenches itself in its own steam or just burns clean to ash. The stages have to be sequenced, with separate controls for each.

That's the shape of your three failure modes right now: not-home, won't-fit, no-elevator are all being discovered by the crew mid-route, in the same step as the delivery itself, the equivalent of trying to cure and char in one burn.

*Concrete move:* split verification from execution into two owners and two timings. A T-24h dispatch pass, done by dispatch, not the delivery crew, confirms three things before a stop is loaded onto tomorrow's route: customer reachable and window confirmed by call or text, freight elevator booked directly with building management (not assumed from the order), and stairwell or doorway clearance checked against the item's listed dimensions with a simple go/no-go template. Only stops that clear the pass get routed. The crew's day becomes pure execution instead of discovery.

**2. Charcoal never bonds, it just sits, which makes fixing it a timing problem.**

Charcoal doesn't absorb into paper, it's loose particulate held on the surface by friction alone. That's why it's fixed immediately, in several light passes over the vulnerable areas, rather than one blanket coat applied later after it's already smudged into whatever state it's in by then.

Your stairwell scuffs have the same "already too late" structure: a single end-of-job photo can't separate old wear from new because the ambiguity was already set well before anyone thought to document it.

*Concrete move:* at the actual chokepoints per job, stairwell turns, doorframes, elevator interior, take a timestamped photo on arrival before any contact and again on departure, filed against that stop's manifest number. The record has to exist before contact happens at the specific points a scuff would occur, not as one photo tacked on at the end.

**3. Activated charcoal is manufactured on a schedule, not found.**

Raw charcoal and activated charcoal aren't the same material. Activation is a distinct, standardized processing step, steam or chemical, that converts ordinary char into something with the porosity to actually do the adsorption job, and it happens ahead of demand, not in response to it.

Your peak-weekend shortage has the same shape: you're hunting for people who already have the quality you need (trusted with a customer's furniture and stairwell) instead of running the process that produces it.

*Concrete move:* stop searching for already-vetted second crew members the week before a peak weekend, and instead run a standing half-day "activation" pass, a scored checklist covering elevator and stairwell handling, tie-down technique, and one supervised live delivery, that a wider pool of temp-agency or seasonal labor works through weekly ahead of forecasted peak dates. That builds a small reserve of activated floaters on a schedule instead of scrambling for vetted ones the week of.
