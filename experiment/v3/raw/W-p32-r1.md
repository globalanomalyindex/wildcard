Drawing on photosynthesis for this one, viewed through what plant physiologists call the constraints-and-limits picture: not what a leaf can absolutely gather, but what actually gates it moment to moment.

**1. The limiting factor never sits still.**
A leaf's photosynthetic rate isn't capped by whichever input is most abundant, it's capped by whichever single one is scarcest right now (light at dawn, CO2 or temperature by midday) — pumping more of a non-limiting factor does nothing. Your Saturday numbers (8% waste and two shorted cafes, same shift) read like exactly that: not a capacity shortfall but a moving bottleneck, a different single resource binding hour to hour and product to product. Concrete move: for one week, tag every late, wasted, or short batch with the *one* resource that actually gated it that hour — oven deck slot, cold-room space, or missing order info. My bet is the tags won't cluster on one culprit; they'll rotate, which tells you "add capacity" is the wrong lever and "route around whichever thing is binding tonight" is the right one.

**2. Two competing users of one aperture usually means it's time to split them in time, not space.**
Most plants pay a real cost to gather carbon: opening stomata for CO2 also loses water, so the two needs fight over the same opening at the same hours. CAM plants (cacti, succulents) sidestep this by opening stomata only at night to fix carbon, then running the light-dependent steps by day when the stomata are shut — decoupling the two processes in time instead of making them share a window. Your cold room has the identical shape: lamination cooling and bread proofing wanting the same space in the same hours. Concrete move: check whether proofing actually needs to run during the hours lamination is chilling, or whether that's just always been the schedule. If laminated dough can go into the cold room in an earlier or later slot, shift it so the room has one tenant per hour instead of two competing ones.

**3. Only one half of the machinery needs the live input.**
Photosynthesis splits into light reactions, which need photons hitting chlorophyll *right now*, and the Calvin cycle, which fixes carbon using stored ATP and NADPH and can run without concurrent light. The two are decoupled: only one leg needs a live signal, the other runs off what's already been captured. Your head baker's schedule is probably the same mix. Concrete move: sort tonight's decisions into two piles — the ones that genuinely need his live judgment because they depend on information that only exists in the moment (a cafe's last-minute text), and the ones that could run off a pre-set rule instead (fixed par levels per SKU, pre-committed dough counts). If the second pile is big, write it down as a standing protocol so it doesn't need him present at all, and let him work only the nights where live, in-the-moment decisions actually cluster. That's the shape of a real four-night week, not just a reduced one.
