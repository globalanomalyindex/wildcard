## angles
- A whole colony almost never crosses its own growth threshold; fifty coin-sized fragments each cross theirs in days. Cutting changes which threshold fires and how fast. -> Your events table is one colony: autovacuum's dead-tuple trigger has to accumulate across all ~600GB before it fires, making the cleanup job a slow operation contending with live traffic all day. -> Partition the table by time (weekly or monthly ranges — `pg_partman` will manage this on PG13): each hot partition crosses its threshold at a small absolute row count and gets vacuumed in place fast, while older partitions barely accumulate dead tuples. Partition pruning is automatic. Since you only get Sunday nights, create the partitioned table alongside the original, dual-write new rows into both, then migrate historical data one partition (oldest first) per Sunday window rather than one 600GB rewrite.

- Tide windows for working the nursery aren't fixed — they slide about 50 minutes later every day and swing through a two-week spring/neap cycle, so a maintenance slot picked by calendar has drifted out from under you. -> The failure isn't picking a bad window — it's trusting a fixed clock time instead of re-checking where the real quiet period sits today. -> Pull hourly load and latency telemetry split by customer region for the last few weeks, find where the actual trough is now (not a year ago, and not assuming it's still nighttime-anchored given EU growth), and move the reporting job there. Put a recurring quarterly check on the calendar — the trough will keep moving as the EU book of business grows.

- A fragment that's done healing gets outplanted onto the reef promptly — left on the nursery tree, it keeps costing light and flow that the next cohort needs. -> Data that's aged out of active use still costs you if it stays in the working set. -> Once a partition ages past your 90-day aggregation window, detach it and move it off the hot tablespace (or just detach it — `pg_partman`'s retention policy can do this on a schedule). It shrinks what your buffer cache and autovacuum are actively carrying.
