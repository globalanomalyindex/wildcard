## angles
- In water gilding, technique lives in written recipes so the master only judges, not executes routine -> the head baker's schedule is stuck in his head, so every night costs him equally regardless of volatility -> write routine as a playbook (par counts by cafe/day, station assignments, autopilot items) to free him from predictable nights, focusing his 4 nights on the 2–3 genuinely volatile ones (Friday into Saturday likely)
- Gilding studios maintain separate zones for wet and dry work because time-slicing one bench causes contamination -> cold room and proofing space create the same incompatibility between opposite environments -> price out physical decoupling (curtained cold cabinet or dedicated proofer cart); if it works financially, the conflict stops needing a schedule
- Rabbit-skin glue can only be reheated a limited number of times before the collagen weakens; damage comes from repeated re-melting, not working late -> the 10pm cutoff targets when changes land rather than how many times the plan gets re-melted between voicemail and mixing -> lock the draft plan at a fixed earlier point (9pm) with a two-amendment cap; after that, late orders are a tomorrow problem, forcing the actual negotiation to happen at 9pm where there is slack rather than 11pm where there isn't
