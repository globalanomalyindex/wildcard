## angles
- Hospitals manage competing demand for limited OR/equipment by assigning fixed block time by day rather than renegotiating daily -> The oven and cold-room hours face the same conflict between bread and laminated dough -> Assign oven/cold-room hours by day-of-week in advance, trading flexibility for a schedule nobody has to hold in their head.

- Airlines accept months of bookings but limit last-minute chaos with confirmed (locked-in) vs. standby (flex-buffer) tiers -> The bakery's constant renegotiation and simultaneous shortage and waste complaints reflect undifferentiated handling of all demand -> Set a hard order cutoff at 6–7pm; late requests fill from a small pre-sized flex batch, not a renegotiated full production plan.

- Ant colonies coordinate without any individual holding the full plan; pheromones in the environment carry the memory instead -> The head baker is a single point of failure; the schedule exists only in his head, making delegation feel dangerous rather than just operationally different -> Externalize the schedule onto a shared artifact (whiteboard grid, doc) so the whole shift can read and mark it up; knowledge survives his stepping back.

- Manufacturing theory protects the bottleneck by decision-ordering: plan the scarce resource first, then build everything else around it -> The bakery's bottleneck (two ovens + cold room) has its schedule effectively set by whoever texts last at 9:58pm -> Decide the oven/cold-room plan first; treat late orders as requests to fit into remaining buffer, not as edits the constraint has to absorb.
