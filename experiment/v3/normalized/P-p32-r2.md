## angles
- Farm CSAs solve the same problem (fixed product, high guessing cost) with default standing orders auto-confirmed unless opted out by deadline -> Currently every loaf is same-day guess with 8% waste from reactive guessing -> Default weekly order per cafe based on trailing average, auto-confirmed unless changed by 6pm two days prior
- Theory of Constraints: bottleneck (ovens) should drive backward-scheduled buffers for everything else, not the other way around -> Cold room and proofing compete for same window because plan flows forward from "when orders come in" not "when ovens need full" -> Map backward from oven capacity and lock laminated-dough/proofing into two non-overlapping blocks by design
- Ship captains and flight controllers externalize decision rules rather than relying on next person reconstructing mental model -> Head baker holding entire schedule in his head is single point of failure blocking nights reduction -> Externalize decision rules before dropping a night (e.g., "if cafe X unconfirmed by 9pm, default to last week minus 10%")
- Job scheduling on shared compute uses fixed blocks (commit time) when two processes contend for one resource, preventing thrashing -> Two ovens and cold room fought over by bread and laminated dough has identical structure; current 10pm cutoff to 11pm mix shows problem -> Treat reserved capacity as truly non-negotiable, same way scheduler won't let low-priority job pre-empt one already running
