## angles
- Bloomberg terminals are famously ugly, dense, information-dense, and users fight to keep them that way — their defense isn't of good design but of sunk-cost fluency and muscle memory -> veterans defending the current layout are protecting years of fluency in exact pixel positions, not defending the design itself -> build per-user saved column configs/views (dense "veteran" preset, roomier "onboarding" preset, curated "demo" preset) so different populations pick their own lens onto the same data instead of one layout negotiating a single solution

- Air traffic control solved a nearly identical problem (dozens to hundreds of tracked objects, experts detecting exceptions instantly) using preattentive symbolic coding: color, shape, and motion carry state so controllers detect anomalies by pop-out, not by reading -> the 11px-and-18-columns approach assumes exception-spotting requires literal reading, but reading is slow and eye-straining because trained perception actually works via pattern recognition, not reading -> prototype where the 3-5 columns dispatchers scan get a compact glyph/color signature per row state instead of text, so pattern recognition does the heavy lifting

- Chase & Simon's chess studies found grandmasters perceive boards in learned chunks meaningless to novices, instantly legible to experts; chunking takes exactly this kind of months-long exposure to build regardless of board design -> the 3-month ramp might be chunk-formation time rather than a legibility problem; if so, no amount of resizing will shortcut it -> build a deliberate chunking scaffold for new hires — a training mode that overlays plain-language labels on the exact same dense layout trainees graduate into, so ramp-up is explicit skill-building rather than an accidental tax

- A diner regular wants a wall of 200 items because they already know what they want and scanning fast is the whole point; a first-time tourist wants a curated 12-item menu, and restaurants print both -> the sales team is trying to close small fleets using the artifact built for 200-load power users — that's not a dispatch-screen flaw, it's showing someone shopping for a sedan the cockpit of a race car -> treat the sales-demo view as an intentionally different product surface (a "starter" dispatcher view scaled to 10-20 loads) rather than a compromise on the real screen, giving small fleets a legitimate on-ramp instead of a screen they're afraid of
