## angles
- two disagreeing sources with lost provenance -> version-control problem, not genealogy -> attach one-line citations (source, location) to each conflicting claim before trying to resolve
- some facts are stable (digital tree, subscriptions) while others decay (elderly relatives' memories, unlabeled photos) -> perishability matters more than importance -> sort to-do by what disappears without action (photo sessions with elders, memory capture) vs. what stays indefinitely (tree merging, subscriptions)
- treating elderly relatives as a lookup table (naming faces) produces short, low-context answers -> oral-history framing unlocks tangential genealogical gold (nicknames, feuds, migration, who-married-whom) -> record relatives narrating freely through photos rather than rushing through labeled interrogation
- can't read Polish, records feel like a wall -> parish records follow rigid templates like legal forms, not prose; pattern-matching works without fluency -> use a genealogy-specific Polish/Latin glossary for the ~30 recurring terms to extract names, dates, relationships
