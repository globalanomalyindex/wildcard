## angles
- Air traffic control displays pack state through visual-weight encoding (position/motion for routine, color/blink/shape for exceptions), not smaller text -> veterans pattern-match shape and color drift rather than reading 18 columns fluently -> instrument veterans' actual eye-movement; real information density may be far lower than 18 columns implies; the table may be a security blanket

- Type designers solved tiny-size legibility with hinting and fonts like Verdana or tabular figures -> dashboard fonts optimize for prose, not dense numeric/alpha scanning at 11px; this is a typography problem -> test screen-optimized or tabular-numeral font alone before redesigning layout; may shrink fluency time and eye-strain complaints

- Flight-booking sites offer dense grids for power users and simplified modes for casual searchers alongside each other, same data -> current prototype failed by replacing the veteran view instead of sitting beside it -> pitch roomier layout as sales-demo and onboarding mode with "switch to expert view" toggle; solves sales-demo without asking veterans to give up anything

- Low-vision and ergonomics research attributes eye-strain to contrast ratio, flicker/refresh, and font weight, not point size alone -> people compensate fine for small-but-crisp text but struggle with small-and-low-contrast -> audit color contrast ratios and grid re-render/flicker behavior separate from font-size; may relieve eye-strain without touching 18-column layout
