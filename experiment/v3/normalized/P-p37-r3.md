## angles
- Blood banks solved traceability and temperature gaps through single barcodes at intake carrying lot/source/time, scanned at every handoff -> Your traceability and temperature gaps are the same gap showing up twice; single-scan approach eliminates later investigation -> Put barcode on case at supplier, scan on dock arrival and departure to timestamp excursion risk
- Vaccine and pharma shippers solved temperature records as a hardware problem (disposable loggers in cases) rather than a process/behavioral problem -> You're trying to police dock behavior when the real solution travels with the shipment -> Put one logger per pallet or supplier lot for on-demand excursion reporting instead of reconstructing records from memory
- ER mass-casualty triage tags encode urgency by color in seconds, with sorting happening by tag color rather than sequential processing -> Your dock sort is the failure point (flood-of-simultaneous-inputs problem), not the road -> Color-tag cases by perishability/speed as they arrive, so sort prioritizes what spoils first and keeps perishables less time in the danger zone
- Airlines release inventory in shrinking windows to standing buyer lists as deadline nears rather than waiting until gate close -> Your day-two discount to fish market is fixed, late, all-or-nothing (same shape as unsold-seat problem) -> Send same-day flash alert late day-one to price-sensitive rotating buyer list before full spoilage risk bakes in; recover more than dump price and get earlier signal on overbuying
