## angles
- Your dock accumulates boxes faster than they can be triaged to routes (like ER patients in hallways) -> Similar bottleneck where time-critical items accumulate risk -> Triage by time since landing or shelf-life-remaining so perishable lots get sorted and moved first, not by arrival order
- Airlines solved chain-of-custody cheaply with barcode tags scanned at each handoff, not expensive sensors -> Your traceability problem has the same structure -> Use $20 barcode/QR labels per lot, scanned at receiving/sort/loadout with phone app for full chain of custody without paper invoices
- Blood banks solve legal temperature-record requirements with cheap irreversible time-temperature indicator stickers that change color at threshold -> You face the same legal/documentation requirement for temperature excursions -> Use $0.50–$1 indicator stickers per case that visually show excursions and provide instant reject/accept signal without instrumenting the dock
- Restaurant expo lines degrade as dishes wait; kitchens solve this with physical FIFO staging -> Your morning sort has the same degradation-vs-sequencing problem -> Stage the dock by "landed earliest" instead of by supplier or destination, forcing oldest product structurally to the sort front
