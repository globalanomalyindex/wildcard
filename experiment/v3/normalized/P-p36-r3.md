## angles
- Hospitals discovered most day-of cancellations trace to predictable causes (patient not cleared, equipment not confirmed, bed not ready) and use mandatory pre-op checklists with 24-48 hour phone confirmation -> Your three failure modes (customer absent, won't fit, elevator unreserved) are knowable in advance, not random -> Implement mandatory pre-delivery call/text confirmation plus measurement/elevator-reservation gate checked before scheduling, so "failure discovered on arrival" becomes "failure discovered on the phone"

- ATC holds planes on the ground where delay is cheap rather than circling at a jammed destination airport where it's expensive -> Your afternoon-window misses show crews absorbing schedule slip stop-by-stop instead of the slip being absorbed at dispatch -> Build slack into the morning schedule (even a static rule like "AM route gets 20% slack, PM gets none") so drift doesn't compound into afternoon cliffs

- Package-theft problem was solved with cheap photo-on-completion proof as routine, not surveillance -> Stairwell-damage disputes (3% of deliveries, unknowable who's responsible) are structurally the same problem -> Phone photo of walls before and after, timestamped, made routine for crews—costs nothing, protects both sides, works like the "photo of package at door" standard that became routine once it protected drivers as much as customers

- Airbnb solved "not enough good co-hosts" with a light credentialing ladder (shadow a job, get rated by lead hosts, unlock more shifts) instead of traditional hiring-and-training -> Peak-weekend "can't find vetted second crew" is a marketplace trust problem, not a staffing problem -> Treat second-crew as a farm system—let your best regular crews nominate and vouch for people, with a one-shift paid trial rated by the lead crew
