## angles
- Pattern-matching and motion-detection are separate visual pathways; an animal invisible to one is caught by the other. -> Your two symptoms are independent failure modes—cold-user navigation ("can't-locate") and existing-account motor memory ("it-moved")—a fix for one can trip the other. -> Split support into two tags and provide separate fixes: progressive disclosure/search for pattern channel, pinned account-level nav order (no silent reflow) for positional channel.

- A concealment pattern that matches perfectly at one viewing distance looks obviously wrong at another; both are true simultaneously. -> Your nav serves two viewers under two conditions on one surface: new self-serve users scanning 13 unfamiliar items and daily enterprise users with known destinations. -> Ship two default views of the same set: compressed first-run (5–6 items) for self-serve, full 13-item for existing accounts; measure week-4 activation before modifying the shared menu.

- Usage data only measures whether something is used, never whether it should be used or whether conditions have changed. -> Your "all 13 items have daily users" will always be true by construction and structurally cannot audit whether items align with current account mix or acquisition cohorts. -> Tag each item with its originating cohort and demote items below a threshold (e.g., founding-cohort under 2% of active accounts) to sub-nav regardless of usage.
