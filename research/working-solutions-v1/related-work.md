# Outside relations and working solutions: related work and contribution boundary

Author: christopher robin fiore. Primary-source review completed 20 September 2026.

This document grounds the prospective working-solutions study. It reports no result for that study. The approved [design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-20-working-solutions-design.md), committed as `6798e79`, fixes the question: does a supplied outside relation help produce a working implementation when the direct comparator receives the same contract format, public checker and repair opportunity? A literature search can identify collisions and narrow a claim; it cannot establish historical priority by failing to find an exact match.

## What the earlier result motivates

The completed [donor-name study](../transfer-v1/paper.md) did not establish a benefit from adding a correct donor name to an already supplied relation. Its amended primary estimate was −0.015625 qualified new mechanisms per four requested actions, with a 95% interval of [−0.109375, 0.0625]. Most task differences were zero and novelty was relative to a finite generated reference bank. That result does not show that outside relations improve software, nor that they cannot. The new question changes both the intervention and the outcome: source-derived behavioral contracts and executable correctness replace donor-name framing and judged mechanism novelty.

The new system represents a proposed transfer as an input-only applicability predicate and an input/output property, alongside an implementation. Its public gate requires applicability to at least one correct example, acceptance of every applicable correct example, and rejection of at least one incorrect output for an applicable input. This establishes consistency and nonvacuity on those public examples only. The model's proposed property never becomes the independent scoring oracle. A rejected contract still receives the common repair opportunity; final implementation behavior remains the outcome.

## Direct prior art

The rows below identify what is already established and the narrower comparison retained here. Descriptions paraphrase the cited primary publications and official artifacts. Reported performance in those papers has not been reproduced for this project, and their benchmarks are not interchangeable with this one.

| Work | Relevant existing method | Boundary of the present study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CodeMetaAgent**, Akhond and Uddin, November 2025 [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18249v1), [method](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18249v1) | Uses metamorphic relations in specification mutation, review, code/test generation and execution. Its multi-agent pipeline already uses relations to guide coding rather than limiting them to final validation. | Turning a relation into tests and a repaired program is not new. The proposed contrast concerns the additional value of a supplied non-target donor relation under the same direct-contract workflow. |
| **Expecto**, Lee and Heo, PACMPL 10, June 2026 [publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/3808332), [author record](https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/expecto-extracting-formal-specifications-from-natural-language-de/), [official artifact](https://github.com/prosyslab/expecto-artifact) | Extracts modular specifications from natural language using a concise DSL and synthesis. Its artifact evaluates acceptance of correct examples and rejection of incorrect examples. | Executable specification extraction and checking both kinds of examples are prior art. The small common gate here is neither a new synthesis algorithm nor a proof of soundness or completeness. |
| **CodeSpecBench**, Chen and colleagues, April 2026 [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12268v1) | Evaluates executable preconditions and postconditions on valid and invalid behavior at function and repository levels. | Measuring whether a proposed contract accepts good behavior and rejects bad behavior is already established. Here contract admission is intermediate feedback; the primary endpoint evaluates independently scored final implementations. |
| **MR-Coupler**, Xu and colleagues, FSE 2026 [paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10126v2), [publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/3808213) | Finds functionally coupled method pairs in source code, generates metamorphic tests with LLMs, and validates them using test amplification and mutation analysis. | Relations, executable checks and validity filtering are prior art. This study supplies relations from a fixed outside source library rather than discovering coupling within target code. |
| **MetaFOE**, Qian and colleagues, June 2026 [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14164v1) | Generates and integrates metamorphic oracles into existing fuzz drivers to check functional behavior beyond crashes. | LLM-derived relations becoming executable oracles are not new. Our generated properties are not trusted as ground truth and do not define the held-out correctness score. |
| **RuleFlow**, Singh and colleagues, February 2026 [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09051v1), [method](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09051v1) | Converts discovered program optimizations into generalized rewrite rules and deploys them in a compiler wherever applicable. | Reusable transformations with applicability conditions are not a new direction. This study does not learn or validate a deployable rewrite library; it measures a bounded assistance intervention on fresh implementations. |
| **ReproAgent**, Hu and colleagues, August 2026 [paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24291v1) | Carries implementation obligations from papers and evidence from related repositories through persistent contracts, generation and repair. The arXiv record reports acceptance to Findings of EMNLP 2026. | External evidence informing implementation contracts and repair is already present. The distinction here is the kind of evidence, generic donor relations from other domains, and its controlled comparison with identically structured direct derivation. |

Analogical assistance itself also predates this study. Yasunaga and colleagues prompt models to generate relevant examples or knowledge before solving tasks, including code generation. The present library supplies authored donor material instead of asking the model to invent its own analogies; that distinction does not create a new general category of reasoning. [Large Language Models as Analogical Reasoners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01714v3)

Executable transfer is not new at a broader level either. Wang, Wang and Dillig synthesize programs jointly subject to relational specifications using counterexample-guided methods. ASPECT uses language-conditioned remapping of observations to reuse a learned policy across analogous tasks. Neither is the same experimental setup, but both prevent an unrestricted claim that this project first makes analogies or relations operational. [Relational Program Synthesis](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~xwangsd/pubs/oopsla18.pdf), [ASPECT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08355v2)

## Recent contract and admission systems

A further primary-source search included work available through September 2026. Contract2Tool learns tool preconditions and effects from metadata, documentation and execution traces, then uses the resulting contracts in tool filtering. Mnemosyne treats generated workflow actions as proposals subject to deterministic admission and bounded repair. ContrAgent, submitted on 16 September 2026, represents required behavior using assume-guarantee contracts over finite traces and compiles them into deterministic automata for online gating and offline evaluation. These methods address different tasks but further narrow any claim about learned applicability or computational admission. [Contract2Tool](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07904v1), [Mnemosyne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00269v3), [ContrAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18128v1)

The proposed gate is a finite-example filter, with no claim to the temporal formalism or stated proof guarantees of those systems. Replacing a model judgment with deterministic execution makes the observed decision reproducible given the artifact and environment; it does not establish that the specification or examples are correct. Expecto's official artifact documents post-submission compiler fixes that changed some specification classifications. That concrete example supports pinning the interpreter and checker, testing their failure modes, and preserving amendments. [Expecto artifact, result notes](https://github.com/prosyslab/expecto-artifact#notes-on-rq1-results)

## The falsifiable contribution

The prospective contribution is a controlled empirical test of **curated outside conditional relations as coding assistance**. It combines established components to ask a narrower question that the cited studies do not directly answer in this form. The experiment must not be advertised as the first use of analogy for coding, the first executable relation, the first conditional contract, the first nonvacuity gate, or a new family of synthesis algorithms.

The three conditions are:

- **D, direct:** derive the contract from the target task and public examples.
- **R, matched outside:** derive it with one intact source card selected using public structural tags and a frozen tie-break rule.
- **X, shuffled outside:** receive an intact card through a frozen permutation, with the same contract format and checker.

All conditions receive two model calls, the same public checking procedure and one mandatory repair opportunity. Consequently, the primary R−D contrast tests the package of supplied outside information and its matching procedure. It does not isolate the source label, conditional representation, gate or repair step. R−X can test the value of this matching procedure relative to this fixed shuffled assignment, subject to the registered testing gate. Shuffling does not make a source fact false, and a shuffled card can still be useful. A contract-acceptance difference is descriptive evidence about an intermediate artifact, not proof that acceptance caused an implementation gain.

Even a positive primary result would support only the frozen configuration, authored task distribution and fixed donor library under the registered execution limits. Equal calls and response limits do not imply equal realized tokens or compute. A null result would not establish equivalence or refute analogy in general. Hidden tests increase precision within a task; they do not turn 32 authored tasks into thousands of independent problems. The study cannot establish production reliability, visual design quality, autonomous discovery of source relations, or creative benefit from the full Wildcard runtime.

## Source cards and separation from target answers

[cards.json](cards.json) contains 16 donor descriptions. Each separates a generic behavioral relation, assumptions, a failure boundary, a short source fact, primary citations and two to four structural tags. The prose is an authored abstraction of the source, not a quotation or a claim that the source institution endorses a software application. Assumptions delimit the abstraction; a citation alone does not validate transfer to a target task.

The cards reuse the 16 source subjects from the earlier study, with revised generic wording. This fixes a small curated library rather than sampling the population of possible donors. It also means the new library is not an independent replication of source selection. The card-writing agent consulted those source materials and the public design, but did not inspect the new benchmark's task implementations, reference programs, generators or checker files while preparing these cards. That procedural separation reduces direct answer encoding; shared AI-assisted authorship and shared high-level design knowledge remain limitations. No claim of independent human domain review is made.

Card text contains no target task identifiers, named target variables, implementation code, hidden test values or proposed target solutions. Tags are human-readable abstractions for the declared matcher, not evidence of semantic compatibility. The canonical vocabulary is `capacity`, `conservation`, `ordering`, `identity`, `isolation`, `recovery`, `retention`, `thresholds`, `fairness`, `causality`, `visibility`, `propagation`, `uncertainty`, `reversibility`, `aggregation`, and `deadlines`. Not every tag must occur in this library; a tag was not added merely to fill the vocabulary.

The cards retain source-specific limits. Local flock coordination does not imply global fairness or collision freedom; seed retention does not establish viability; a general lighthouse indication does not certify a hazard-free route. The wetland card explicitly labels its finite-capacity balance as an abstraction. Those boundaries are part of the supplied information and must remain intact when cards are shuffled. Primary-source links identify the evidence checked on 20 September 2026; source pages can subsequently change.

The useful research object is the inspectable chain from source assumption to proposed target property, public gate decision, final implementation and independently scored trace. Whether that chain improves the measured outcome remains an empirical question, to be answered by the frozen run rather than by the name of the method.
